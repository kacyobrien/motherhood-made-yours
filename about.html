The beginning

The life I thought I was supposed to live

In 2014, I had just been accepted into medical school. From the outside, it looked like everything was working. I had spent years becoming the person I thought I was supposed to be.

Inside, I was exhausted, burned out, and deeply unhappy.

A conversation with a stranger in a coffee shop led me to a three-day personal growth course. That weekend gave me the space to admit something I had been afraid to say: I didn't want to become a doctor. I wanted to travel. I wanted to write. I wanted to serve people in a way that felt true to me.

So I changed direction.

Learning to hear myself again

Real change began with telling the truth.

I began paying attention to the parts of life I had never been taught to understand: my emotions, my patterns, my inner voice, and the choices that made me feel more like myself.

The years that followed held travel, writing, love, family, and a coaching practice built around the things I once needed to learn for myself. I have now supported hundreds of people who knew they wanted something more, even when they could not yet see the next step.

That work taught me to slow down and listen closely. Real change often begins when we feel safe enough to tell the truth about where we are.

“I feel like who I am, it hasn't changed. It's just I'm back home.”
— Amy
Sharing the message

Turns out others needed to hear this.

Living from my heart turned into speaking, connecting, and sharing - rooms, stages, and conversations with women who heard their own truth in mine. That became its own journey.

Motherhood

Becoming a mama brought that lesson into sharper focus.

For most of my life I moved fast. I was an overachiever, always reaching for the next thing. Over the years I learned to slow down and listen more closely to myself.

During pregnancy, that listening became part of how I explored birth, and for me it led to choosing a home birth. Preparing meant paying attention to my fears, asking questions, and choosing the support that helped me feel safe. My baby girl was born at home, peacefully, in less than eight hours. That is simply how my own story unfolded.

For me, conscious motherhood is not about doing it perfectly. It is about noticing what we have inherited, choosing what still feels true, and making the rest our own.

Becoming a mother was birthing myself too. Choosing myself, choosing to live on my terms, with my intention. And everything I had learned about consciousness and healing - the tools, the techniques - I needed more in motherhood than ever before. That's the woman I'm writing for: the one in the middle of her own maiden-to-mother becoming, who senses there's a slower, truer way and wants someone who's walked it.

There is so much noise around motherhood: how it should look, what a good mother should do, and how quickly we should find our footing.

Motherhood Made Yours is my place for a slower, more honest conversation. It is where I share what I am learning about motherhood, home, healing, intuition, and building a life that fits the woman living it.

I don't have a perfect formula. I bring curiosity, lived experience, and a willingness to sit with the real thing. My hope is that this space helps you hear your own voice more clearly and trust the life you are making.

The podcast

Where these conversations live out loud.

Most weeks it is just my voice: solo episodes about identity, postpartum, loneliness, marriage, family culture, healing, boundaries, and finding your own way through motherhood. Whatever the topic, it comes back to the inner work - who do you need to be here? Birth is part of my own story, and every mother brings a different story of her own.

It matters because motherhood can be lonely, and there is no single right way to live it. Hearing another woman tell the truth about the messy parts, the magic, and the becoming can help us feel less alone. That's the space I want to hold, one honest conversation at a time.
